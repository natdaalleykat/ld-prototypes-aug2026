Foundation expansion: suggest the next product at the moment it solves a problem

Prototype · Natalie Friedman · August 2026 · Reconstructions on current production pages, not screenshots
Premise. 99% of Foundation customers use flags only, even though Experimentation and Observability (including Session Replay) are in the plan they already pay for. Interruption marketing won't move them; the play is to catch the moment when the adjacent product solves the problem in front of them, the value is self-evident, and the friction is provably low (we detect their SDK and their tracked events before suggesting). Each moment below is one of those catches, ordered roughly from highest intent to lowest.
Moment 1: rollout configured, inline EXPERIMENTATION
The user builds a 25/75 percentage rollout. An inline card in the targeting canvas points out they are one metric from an A/B test with this exact targeting, included in their plan.
Moment 2: rollout saved, modal EXPERIMENTATION
Same offer as Moment 1, but as a save-confirmation modal after they commit. They just took a measurable risk in production; peak motivation, higher intrusion. A/B these two.
Moment 3: errors spike on Monitoring SESSION REPLAY
Errors on a flagged rollout, and we detected an o11y-compatible JavaScript SDK. Offer Session Replay as the answer to the problem on screen: watch the session, one line to enable, privacy defaults stated.
Moment 4: the retro insight AI CASE STUDY
Vega scans flag history for experiment-shaped rollouts and writes the case study: "3 of your last 14 rollouts looked like experiments, here's what simplified-onboarding-email could have told you." Drawer pivots to drafting an experiment on the live rollout.
Moment 5: personalized empty state EXPERIMENTATION
The Experimentation page stops marketing and lists their own flags as ready-to-run candidates, each pre-matched to an event they already track. Destination for the deep links in Moments 1, 2, and 4.
Every page has a "Why this moment" button in the bottom switcher with the trigger, the gating rules, and the risk to test. Numbers in the mocks (traffic, lift, error counts) are illustrative.
